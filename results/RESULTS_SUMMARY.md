# MoltBook re-analysis: results summary

Generated by `analysis/s11_assemble.py` from `results/results.json`. Re-create everything with `analysis/run_all.sh`. All random seeds are 42.

## Data

- Primary data: archive revision `ee98f6c8`, the revision pinned by the repo's `moltbook-observatory-archive` gitlink and used by the earlier run. Window 2026-01-28 to 2026-02-20 (UTC): 222,791 posts, 879,629 comments, 90,704 agents in the agents table (73,543 active in the window).
- The current archive head (`2bd2498a`) has back-filled posts (1,500,385 in the window), but only 15.5% of the pinned revision's window comments are still present, so it was not used as primary. Row counts and hashes of both are in `results/data_manifest.json`.
- Interaction graph: 14,542 nodes, 90,574 edges, 154,231 interactions. In the pinned revision only 16.2% of top-level comments point to a post present in it; the rest produce no edge. The `merged` variant (pinned revision plus head, deduplicated by id) raises this to 98.2% and is reported as a sensitivity check.

## 1. Population structure (step 2)

- k-means over k=3..8 on five standardized features (in/out-degree, exact betweenness, clustering, PageRank). Max-silhouette k = **8**, silhouette **0.916**; at k=6 the silhouette is 0.913. The curve is flat (k=3: 0.906, k=4: 0.908, k=5: 0.908, k=6: 0.913, k=7: 0.915, k=8: 0.916).
- Bootstrap (100 resamples): ARI vs reference mean 0.930 (95% 0.712-0.999), silhouette mean 0.907 (0.895-0.919).
- Largest cluster holds 92.9% of agents. 84.0% of agents have no interaction at all and share one identical feature vector; 87.2% have total degree <= 1. The high silhouette therefore measures how far this inactive mass sits from a few active agents. It does not show separated roles among active agents.
- GMM (k=8): silhouette 0.818, ARI vs k-means 0.536, mean max posterior 0.996 (1.2% of agents below 0.9). BIC selects k=8. Soft assignments are nearly hard, so GMM does not describe the roles better; the two partitions agree only moderately.

## 2. Cascades (step 3)

- **Cap status: confirmed.** `rq2_diffusion.py` sorts meme cascades by size and keeps `cascades[:10000]`. With the code's defaults (n=3..5, >= 10 distinct adopters) there are **281,522** meme cascades, so the reported 10,000 is the cap. The smallest capped cascade has 94 adopters. The earlier run used n=3..5 (function default), not the n=2..5 in the paper/config.
- Uncapped, n=2..5: **471,316** meme cascades, plus 286 skill and 6 behavioral (total 471,608). Distinct n-grams by threshold: n=2: >=2 1,410,580, >=5 437,631, >=10 189,794; n=3: >=2 2,504,750, >=5 514,137, >=10 172,223; n=4: >=2 1,935,267, >=5 277,566, >=10 77,305; n=5: >=2 1,208,223, >=5 127,045, >=10 31,994.
- The paper's definition (>= 2 agents) yields millions of cascades dominated by common phrases. The >= 10 adopter rule of the code is kept for every fit.
- Cascade type comes from the extraction method (n-gram = meme, code block = skill, formatting regex = behavioral), not from keyword classification of the seed post's submolt as the paper states.
- Embedding cascades (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, FAISS kNN, connected components at cosine >= tau): tau = 0.82 chosen on a held-out calibration sample (F1 0.887; test F1 0.886, precision 0.930, recall 0.847). 253 cascades with >= 10 adopters (largest component holds 14.1% of items). tau +/- 0.05: tau=0.77: 241, tau=0.82: 253, tau=0.87: 235.

## Open issues

- Primary data choice: the pinned revision reproduces the earlier run, but its graph drops most top-level comments because their posts are missing from it (see Data). The merged variant fixes this and changes the structural numbers (section 9). The authors should decide which to report as primary.
- Q definition: the code's Q differs from the paper's in weights and components; Q_spec's comment-quality formula is defined in this rerun and needs sign-off.
- The paper's single-agent matched baseline is not estimable on the pinned revision (estimable on the merged data); a low-participation baseline is primary, and the Methods text must say which is used.
- Human ratings for Q validation are pending (`results/q_validation_sample.csv`). Agreement statistics will be added by rerunning step 7.
- Shared-frailty Cox is not available in the Python stack; the stratified Cox stands in for it. A gamma-frailty fit (e.g. R coxme) would need a different toolchain.
- The exposure definition in the code is asymmetric between adopters and non-adopters and uses a static, whole-window graph; the logistic and static-Cox results inherit that bias, and only the time-varying Cox avoids it.
- Role taxonomy names in the paper (Amplifier, Curator) have no implementation; the code's rules use different role names.
- Silhouette-based k selection is not identifiable here (flat curve, maximum at the edge of the range).
- The autonomy rules are heuristics without ground truth. R2 (verbatim self-duplication) flags heavy comment spammers, who write most comments.
- The earlier run did not apply the 2026-01-28..2026-02-20 window to posts (it included posts up to 2026-02-24).
- Embedding-cascade threshold is calibrated against a lexical near-duplicate proxy, not human judgements.
- Cascade extraction, exposure models and power law were not rerun on the merged variant (memory: about 2.6M items).
